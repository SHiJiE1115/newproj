\section{The pair-support boundary under gross substitutes}
\label{app:gs-pair-support}
The fee restriction in this appendix is stronger than submodularity.
It changes the pair-support minimum of
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-pair-resource-minimum} from two to three,
without changing the complete-source or full causal-skip comparator
contract. Gross substitutes (GS) is applied to the set function used
as a fee; no demand, reward or posterior model is imposed on the
unknown relational state.

\paragraph*{The standard GS condition in the quadratic class.}
For sets $X,Y\subseteq Q$ and $i\in X\setminus Y$, the discrete
exchange formulation of GS requires
\begin{align}
 F(X)+F(Y)\leq\max\bigl\{&F(X-i)+F(Y+i),\nonumber\\
 &\max_{j\in Y\setminus X}
       [F(X-i+j)+F(Y+i-j)]\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:gs-exchange}
\end{align}
The first option remains when $Y\setminus X$ is empty. Additions and
deletions denote set operations. For the nonnegative pair-coverage
fee~\eqref{eq:global-pair-fee}, expansion gives
\[
 F(S)=\sum_i\left(c_i+\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\right)
                  {\bf1}_{i\in S}
       -\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}{\bf1}_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}.
\]
Its conditional two-coordinate interaction is $w_{ij}$, independent
of the conditioning set. The standard submodularity/S3
characterization therefore says precisely that the minimum of
$w_{ij},w_{ik},w_{jk}$ is attained at least twice for each triple.
We use this established characterization, as recorded in
Hajek~\cite[Definition 3 and Proposition 4]{hajek2008substitutes};
neither the characterization nor its hierarchy geometry is a new
claim of this paper.

\begin{theorem}[GS-restricted pair-support minimum, computer-assisted]
\label{thm:gs-pair-resource-minimum}
Let the source and private zero-error policy classes be those of
Theorem~\ref{thm:global-pair-resource-minimum}, on at most four
indexed pair-equality queries, with unrestricted class count.
Suppose the nonnegative quadratic pair-coverage
fee~\eqref{eq:global-pair-fee} is GS. If at most two distinct pair
weights are positive, then $R_{\mathcal A}=R_{\mathcal P}$.

Over all complete sources with exactly four indexed queries, the
least positive pair support permitting a strict gap is exactly
three. This also holds when all individual charges must be strictly
positive. A complete source and fee with three positive pairs satisfy
\[
 R_{\mathcal P}=\frac{1025}{47},\qquad
 R_{\mathcal A}\leq\frac{324871}{14899},\qquad
 R_{\mathcal P}-R_{\mathcal A}\geq\frac{54}{14899}>0.
\]
No exact adaptive value is asserted for this witness.
\end{theorem}

The support counts positive weights on named groups of size two.
Repeated occurrences of a pair combine into its one weight. It does
not count resources in arbitrary higher-arity representations of
the same function. In particular, this is not an extremal statement
for all GS set functions or all menu sizes.

\begin{proof}
\paragraph*{Why two positive pairs must be disjoint.}
If $w_{ij},w_{jk}>0$ and $w_{ik}=0$, the triple has a unique minimum,
contradicting S3. The positive-edge relation is transitive, so its
connected components are cliques. With at most two positive edges,
there are no edges, one edge, or two disjoint edges. For four labelled
vertices the $64$ support patterns contain $15$ disjoint-clique
patterns, of which $10$ have at most two edges; these finite counts
are also checked by the composer. The transitivity argument, rather
than those counts alone, establishes the required exclusion.

For two disjoint pairs, order their weights as $h_1\geq h_2\geq0$.
Merge the first pair at height $h_1$, the second at height $h_2$,
and both pairs at height $h_3=0$. The weight on a pair of leaves is
the height at which they first meet. The three pair partitions and
two relative weight orders give six ranked balanced histories.
They also contain the one-edge and empty cases on their zero faces.
We verify the larger closed cones $h_1\geq h_2\geq h_3\geq0$;
their $h_3=0$ faces suffice for the two-edge lower bound.

For each individual-price order $o$, use
\[
 c_{o(j)}=d_0+\cdots+d_j\quad(0\leq j<4),\qquad
 (h_1,h_2,h_3)=(d_4+d_5+d_6,d_5+d_6,d_6),
 \quad d\geq0.
\]
Every purchased-set fee is linear in these seven variables. The
$24$ closed price orders include every nonnegative individual
price, with all ties and zeros, and the six balanced histories
include every at-most-two-edge GS fee. This is a continuous cover,
not a collection of tested price vectors.

give $144$ roots. A separate fraction-based reader rebuilds fees

\paragraph*{A lower bound against the entire priority class.}
On $W=(0,1,4,7,9,11,15)$, take the fixed prior
\[
 \pi=(0,0,0,9/47,3/47,13/94,57/94).
\]
Let $B_o(V,k,S)$ be the minimum \emph{unnormalized} prior-weighted
future fee for a fixed global order $o$, current feasible worlds
$V$, suffix position $k$ and already purchased set $S$.
The weights are the original $\pi$, not renormalized on $V$.
For pure or empty $V$ put $B_o=0$. At a mixed state with $k=4$
put $B_o=+\infty$, even if $\pi(V)=0$. Otherwise let $q=o(k)$,
$V_b=\{w\in V:q(w)=b\}$ and $m(V)=\sum_{w\in V}\pi(w)$.
Then
\begin{align}
 B_o(V,k,S)=\min\bigl\{&B_o(V,k+1,S),\nonumber\\
 &m(V)\bigl(F(S+q)-F(S)\bigr)
       +\sum_{b=0}^1 B_o(V_b,k+1,S+q)\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:gs-fullclass-bellman}
\end{align}
The first option is SKIP. The second is feasible only if both
branches are feasible; it includes uninformative purchases.
Correctness is imposed on every legal world, including zero-mass
ones. A pure state may stop at no greater monotone cost. Every
fixed-order causal policy makes precisely one of these choices,
and $S$ retains all context needed for a nonadditive final-set fee.
Induction on the suffix proves the recurrence. Randomization
cannot improve a specified-prior minimum of linear expected costs.

The exact proof and its separate fraction-based reader evaluate
all $24$ orders and $1602$ distinct states: $526$ mixed choices,
$572$ pure states and $504$ infeasible mixed terminal states.
There are $110$ mixed zero-prior states, which retain their
correctness obligations. They certify
\[
 B_{\mathcal P}(\pi,F)
    =\min_o B_o(W,0,\varnothing)=\frac{1025}{47}.
\]
This point lower bound does not assume that the cached $32$
priority profiles exhaust the class. It directly covers every
global order and causal continuation.

Mix $T_6,T_{18},T_{19},T_{20}$ with probabilities
$(14,6,24,3)/47$. Its costs on all seven worlds are
\[
 (989,989,983,1025,1025,1025,1025)/47.
\]
The maximum matches the full-class prior lower bound, so
$R_{\mathcal P}=1025/47$.

\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A relative GS neighborhood with a full-class lower bound]
\label{cor:gs-relative-robustness}
Keep this source, let $z=(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,v)$, and set
$w_{12}=w_{13}=w_{23}=v$ and all other pair weights to zero.
For every $z$ in the closed box
\[
 \|z-(7,4,3,6,4)\|_\infty\leq\frac{27}{208586},
\]
the fee is GS, all individual charges and exactly three pair
weights are positive, and
$R_{\mathcal P}(z)-R_{\mathcal A}(z)\geq27/14899>0$.
This is a relative five-parameter family, not independent
perturbations of all ten quadratic coefficients.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each purchased set $S$, the five nonnegative coefficients
of $F_z(S)$ sum to at most $4+3=7$. Thus if
$\|z-z^0\|_\infty\leq r$, then
$|F_z(S)-F_{z^0}(S)|\leq7r$ for \emph{every} $S$.
For any legal priority policy, prior averaging preserves that
bound. Taking the minimum over the full policy class gives
$B_{\mathcal P}(\pi,z)\geq1025/47-7r$.
For the explicit adaptive mixture above, the maximum over all
worlds is at most $324871/14899+7r$. Hence
\[
 R_{\mathcal P}(z)-R_{\mathcal A}(z)
   \geq\frac{54}{14899}-14r
   \geq\frac{27}{14899}
\]
at the stated radius. Positivity and the fixed equal-weight
triangle preserve GS throughout the closed box. This standard
Lipschitz argument uses the full-class Bellman center bound,
not exhaustiveness of cached priority profiles. The composer
also checks all $16$ set sensitivities and the $224$ older affine
interval inequalities as consistency checks; the latter are
not a completeness premise for this proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Prior objectives, established tools and scope}
The same prior supplies a distinct comparison:
$T_{39}^{\mathcal A}$ has prior expectation $1022/47$, whereas
$B_{\mathcal P}(\pi,F)=1025/47$. Its Bayes gap is at least $3/47$;
this is not the worst-input gap of the theorem. It also obstructs
one specific transfer route to independent-reward submodular-cost
Pandora search~\cite[Theorem 4.1, arXiv version]{pandora}.
If a target simultaneously had optimal adaptive utility
$K-B_{\mathcal A}$ and optimal fixed-prefix utility
$K-B_{\mathcal P}$ for one common $K$, that theorem's utility
equality would force Bayes cost equality here, a contradiction.
This conditional argument excludes neither other reductions nor
other reward-search models. The cited theorem numbering refers
to the inspected arXiv v1, without asserting that its body equals
the publisher's final article.

Nor is a generic GS adaptivity gap new: additive functions already
satisfy GS, and stochastic Boolean evaluation has known additive
gaps~\cite{hellerstein2022adaptivity,nielsen2025kofn}.
Theorem 1 of Nielsen et al. gives a tight gap of two for product-bit
$k$-of-$n$ evaluation. At a mixed feasible state of a full-cube
threshold, every unqueried bit is pivotal on some completion;
permanently skipping it cannot give a correct policy. Thus the
causal-SKIP convention does not erase that known phenomenon.
The candidate increment here is the exact pair-support boundary
on complete four-query sources under the stated private minimax
contract, compared with unrestricted minimum two. It is not a
new GS model, general adaptivity principle or new Bellman method.
The bounded primary comparison does not settle priority of that
exact boundary or rule out every preserving reduction.

GS of a fee also differs from adaptive submodularity of a
posterior utility. Golovin and Krause's definition and named
guarantees~\cite[Definition 3 and Theorem 5]{golovin2017adaptive}
concern conditional expected marginal utility; the cited
nonuniform-cost extension uses modular item costs. Those
approximation guarantees do not supply an exact equality of
the two cost-minimizing classes considered here. This comparison
does not rule out a separately proved utility reduction.
