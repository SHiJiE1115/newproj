binary label is the COMPLETE fibre of all equivalence partitions satisfying
its finite conjunction of SAME and DIFFERENT constraints. Records may be
shared by queries or hidden from them. The number of equivalence classes is
unrestricted. Assume both label fibres are nonempty and their projected query
supports are disjoint, so a zero-error decision is possible. Public source
constraints and finite nonnegative additive query prices are known.

Let R_A be the minimum, over zero-error adaptive policies with input-independent
private coins, of the maximum over promised inputs of the expected actual
purchased-query cost. Let R_P use the FULL baseline: each seed may choose a
different global query order and history-dependent permanent skips/stopping;
the two answer branches share that seed's order. Then R_A=R_P for every such
source and every finite nonnegative real additive price vector.

This is a worst-input expected-cost theorem, not equality for each known
input prior, not a per-seed hard budget, not an independent-bit distribution
assumption, and not an equality for arbitrary partial Boolean promises.
Overlapping two-label supports make both classes infeasible and are excluded
from the finite-cost statement; constant-label tasks are trivially cost0.
