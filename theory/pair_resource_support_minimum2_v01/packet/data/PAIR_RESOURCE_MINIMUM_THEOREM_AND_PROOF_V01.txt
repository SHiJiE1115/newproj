PAIR-RESOURCE SUPPORT MINIMUM ON THE FIXED FOUR-QUERY SOURCE1209
New internal result; not yet integrated into or published with V22.

1. Precise claim and model

There are four reliable equality queries q_i=(2i,2i+1) on eight records,
i=0,1,2,3. The two promised label families have prior SAME blocks
  label0: {0,1,2,4,6}, {3,5}, {7}, and prior DIFFERENT(3,7);
  label1: {0,2,4,6}, {1,3,7}, {5}, with no prior DIFFERENT constraint.
Take every coarsening of each three-block prior consistent with its DIFFERENT
constraint. There are three negative and five positive physical partitions.
Their query words, w=sum_i 2^i answer(q_i), project to
  W0={1,7,9}, W1={0,4,11,15}, W=(0,1,4,7,9,11,15).
No additional arbitrary Boolean words are admitted or deleted.

A is the class of zero-error adaptive policies. P permits a single global order
per deterministic seed, shared across both branches, with causal permanent SKIP
and stopping. A randomized policy is an input-independent private mixture of
legal zero-error seeds. Both objectives are the maximum, over all original
inputs, of the seed expectation of the final purchased-set fee. Write them RA
and RP. No claim about equality under every Bayes prior is made.

Consider only the additive-plus-pair-coverage family
  F(S)=sum_{i in S} c_i + sum_{e in E} w_e 1{S intersects e},
where c_i>=0, w_e>0, and E consists of distinct two-element subsets of
{0,1,2,3}. There is no extra global setup resource. Define pair support as |E|;
repeated copies of the same pair combine into a single coefficient.

THEOREM. On this fixed source, the minimum pair support allowing RA<RP is exactly
two. The upper bound is attained with every c_i strictly positive. For every
one-pair choice e and all c_i,lambda>=0,
  F(S)=sum_{i in S}c_i+lambda 1{S intersects e} implies RA=RP.
This is a sourcewise minimum in the stated cost family. It is not a lower bound
over all four-query sources or for arbitrary shared-resource group sizes.

3. Universal one-pair lower bound: finite proof of a continuous statement

Fix e and one of the 24 permutations o of the individual prices. Parametrize
  c_{o(j)}=d0+...+dj, j=0,1,2,3, and lambda=d4,
where d lies in the nonnegative five-dimensional orthant. These closed cones
cover every nonnegative c,lambda, including zero, equal-price and lambda=0
boundaries. Every purchased-set fee and every difference of two such fees is
linear in d. Nonnegativity on all generators of a cone proves nonnegativity
throughout it; no numerical minimax game is solved at a generator point.

The certificate uses the original P32 followed by A40 as 72 columns. Adding P
columns does not change the adaptive game, since P is already a subclass of A.
Within a cone, the following rules are sufficient.

(a) Full-game saddle. For an original input w and original priority p, let h be
its paid mask on w. Check that every original A column costs at least F(h) on w,
and that p costs at most F(h) on all seven inputs. Then both values equal F(h).
Only four fixed templates are tested: (w=1,P6), (w=7,P6), (w=9,P19),
(w=1,P19). Even in a descendant cone these inequalities refer to the full game.

(b) Weak dominance. Delete a column if a surviving column is no dearer on all
surviving rows; delete a row if another surviving row is no cheaper on every
surviving column. Each operation preserves the finite mixed minimax value.
All inequalities are universal linear inequalities on the current cone.

(c) Full-original-row P lifting. For each surviving terminal column t, exhibit
one original legal P column p_t no dearer on ALL SEVEN original inputs. This
last check is not restricted to terminal objective rows. Row inequalities
recorded during deletion continue to hold on the subset of terminal columns.
By induction through row deletions, for any terminal mixture every deleted row
is bounded by a terminal row. An optimal terminal mixture, extended by zero to
the original columns, therefore has the original adaptive value on all inputs.
Replace each terminal column by p_t with the same private probability. The
all-original-row inequalities show RP<=RA. Inclusion P subset A gives RA<=RP.
The argument also applies along nested cone refinements: inherited column
subsets preserve all earlier row inequalities. It does not assert that weak
dominance started separately from A and P reaches the same fixed point.

When these rules do not close a cone, split on a recorded linear form f into
the two CLOSED halves f>=0 and f<=0. This changes no task, policy or fee family.
For parent generators r, retain nonnegative/zero generators and, for each
positive p and negative n, add the boundary generator
  f(p)n-f(n)p.
These generate the nonnegative half: in a conic decomposition, cancel every
negative f contribution against positive f contributions, leaving nonnegative
generators and the displayed pairwise boundary combinations. The other half
is identical after negating f. Hence their union covers the parent cone.

The cone is pointed (contained in d>=0) and full five-dimensional; every actual
split has generators of both signs. For the complete accumulated inequality
description, a feasible nonzero ray is extreme iff its active normals have
rank four. Thus removing only candidate rays whose active rank is below four
preserves all extreme rays and the cone they generate. The separate reader
reconstructs BOTH halves and verifies exact generator sets, active ranks and
full rank five, using Fraction Gauss-Jordan instead of the producer's primitive
integer elimination. This is standard polyhedral reasoning, not a claimed new
general optimization algorithm.

Result: all 6*24=144 parameter roots are covered by 164 nodes and 10 splits,
with 154 closed leaves: 73 full-game saddles and 81 dominance/lifting leaves.
There are 5,533 column deletions, 342 row deletions and 252 full-original-row
P-lifts. Every node is reachable exactly once; there are no residual leaves,
unstarted roots or budget-success substitutions. The separate reader checked
223,769 scalar generator inequalities and 289 exact rational ranks.

The retained V01 attempt closed only 136 roots using dominance/lifting alone.
Its 113 residual leaves were insufficient certificates, not counterexamples.
V02 added the four already established additive-case saddle templates; the
fee family and original game did not change. Both attempt records remain.
The universal result includes lambda=0, so it also excludes pair support zero.

4. Two pairs suffice: one frozen algebraically derived rational witness

Take c=(4,3,2,3), w_{1,2}=w_{2,3}=2, with all other pair weights zero.
The complete fee vector in masks 0..15 is
  (0,4,5,9,6,10,9,13,5,9,10,14,9,13,12,16).
It is a coverage function with six positive resources: four singleton resources
and two pair resources. Its smallest proper query marginal is two, so it is
strictly increasing and positive on every nonempty set. It is submodular, but
is NOT strictly submodular on every incomparable pair of sets.

For T_i(low,high), let u=T_0(1,0), v=T_1(1,0), z=T_3(u,0). Use five old trees:
  a1=T_1(u,T_3(0,1)); a2=T_3(u,T_1(0,1));
  a3=T_2(T_1(z,1),T_3(v,1));
  p3=T_2(T_1(u,1),T_3(v,1)); p4=T_2(T_1(z,1),T_3(u,1)).
Priority orders for a1,a2,p3,p4 are respectively (1,0,2,3), (2,3,0,1),
(2,3,1,0), (2,1,3,0). Their costs in W order are
  a1:  9, 9, 9,10, 9,10,10
  a2:  9, 9, 9, 9,10,10,10
  a3: 16,16,12,12,12, 9, 9
  p3: 13,13,12,12,13, 9, 9
  p4: 16,16,13,13,12, 9, 9.

An adaptive mixture gives weights (13,12,3,1)/29 to (a1,a2,a3,p3).
Its costs on all seven inputs are
  (286,286,273,286,286,286,286)/29.
The prior (0,1,0,3,3,11,11)/29 gives every one of the original 40 A columns
expected cost at least 286/29. Equality holds exactly for A6,A20,A21,A39.
Consequently RA=286/29.

A priority mixture gives weights (7,7,1,1)/16 to (a1,a2,p3,p4).
Its costs are (155,155,151,158,158,158,158)/16.
The prior (0,0,0,1,1,3,3)/8 gives every original 32 P column cost at least
79/8. Equality holds exactly for P6,P18,P19,P30. Consequently RP=79/8.
The exact gap is 79/8-286/29=3/232>0. The ratio RP/RA is 2291/2288;
the relative cost reduction versus P in this witness is 3/2291, about 0.13%.
This small separation is a structural existence/minimality result, not evidence
of a large application improvement.

All fractions, the fee and the certificate were frozen before full-column
evaluation. They came from balancing these five old profiles: write the four
cheap pair fees as p=9, F10=p+h with h=1, F14=p+L with L=3,
F7=F13=p+L+k with k=1, and F15=p+M with M=7=2L+k. Solving the displayed
binding linear equalities gives the stated mixtures and priors. Only this
single fee point was evaluated; there was no LP, price/prior grid or retuning.

5. Catalogue-free separate lower-bound check

The separate checker reads ONLY the frozen candidate, not the policy families,
the main result or the cone producer. A read audit enforces this for research
artifact inputs. It reconstructs all eight physical partitions and seven words.
For a feasible world set X and purchased set S, a label-pure state stops at
mass(X)F(S). An impure state must query even when mass(X)=0. The adaptive
recurrence minimizes the sum of the two successor values over every unbought
query. Under the frozen A prior it gives 286/29 using 63 memoized states.

For each of the 24 global orders, a second recurrence either permanently skips
the next query or purchases it and uses the same remaining order on both
branches. It stops only on a label-pure state. The minimum under the frozen P
prior is 79/8. A private mixture of seeds cannot beat a fixed-prior deterministic
Bayes minimum. Combining these lower bounds with the all-input upper mixtures
proves both randomized worst-input values directly. Zero-mass inputs remain
correctness requirements everywhere. These are fixed-prior certificates, not
an assumption that the A and P Bayes values agree for all priors.

6. Derived sparse robustness

For fixed policy classes, changing every fee by at most r changes each class's
optimal worst-input value by at most r: it changes every seed/input cost by
at most r, hence every private expectation, input maximum and policy infimum
by at most r. Therefore RP(G)-RA(G)>=3/232-2r.
At r=3/928 the gap is at least 3/464.

Perturb the FOUR individual prices and the TWO positive pair weights each by
at most delta=1/1856. A set hits at most six resources, so its fee changes by
at most 6delta=3/928. All six resource weights remain strictly positive, the
family remains strictly increasing coverage/submodular, and the gap remains
at least 3/464. This is a six-dimensional box in the stated sparse resource
parameters. It is not a full fifteen-dimensional strict-submodular fee box.
